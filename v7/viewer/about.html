Forty independent C. elegans brains, each one a faithful port of the worm's actual 302-neuron connectome, each in its own private world, each eating a great text — Hamlet, the Tao Te Ching in Legge's English and in the original 道德經, and Beowulf. What they eat becomes what they write. What they write is judged, and the best writers' brains seed the next generation.

What you are looking at

Every worm is a separate simulation of C. elegans, one of the few animals whose entire nervous system has been mapped, neuron by neuron, synapse by synapse. We use that real connectome — every cell, every connection — and we drop each worm into its own private copy of a text. Words drift across the worm's world; the worm smells them, steers toward them, eats them. The sequence of words a worm eats is its poem. The worms live in four flasks of ten, one flask per text; each flask is a separate evolving lineage with its own judge.

Each worm runs continuously, independently. Nobody has to watch — they keep writing whether you're here or not.

How a worm reads a word

Real C. elegans sense their chemical environment through 12 pairs of amphid sensilla in the nose — ASE, AWA, AWB, AWC, ASH, ASI, ASJ, ASK, ASG, ADL, ADF, AFD — left and right halves of each pair allow the worm to tell which side a smell is coming from. That bilateral asymmetry is the basis of all its navigation.

Every word emits a 12-channel chemical signature into those neuron pairs, but the signature is made differently per text:

  • Hamlet — every unique word in the play (~4,500) is embedded with nomic-embed-text and reduced to 11 dimensions with UMAP: an unsupervised geometry of Shakespeare's vocabulary, one dimension per neuron pair.
  • The Tao Te Ching and Beowulf — each word is rated 0–1 on 11 named semantic axes (dread, warmth, nature, body, motion, power, the sacred, speech, time, conflict, stillness) by a large language model reading the word in its sense in that text. So sea carries a trace of dread in Beowulf and none in the Tao; 道 smells of the sacred, not of roads. Named axes mean you can read the desire heatmap: a worm following channel 10 is literally following the smell of quietness.
  • The 12th channel is grammar. For the English texts it scores how well a candidate word's part of speech continues the part-of-speech sequence of the last words eaten (a trigram trained on the text itself). For 道德經 it is a character trigram: how expected is this character after the two just eaten — the text's own phrase structure as a smell. Punctuation smells of grammar and nothing else, so a worm can learn to end a sentence.

The direction a word is in (left of the worm, right of it, dead ahead) scales the L vs R member of each neuron pair asymmetrically. Same word, different position, different steering signal.

Hunger, and death

A worm is born peckish and gets hungrier every second. Eating a word feeds it; going without empties it in about ten sim-minutes. Hunger is not decoration: a hungry worm smells harder and moves faster (the roaming state real starved C. elegans enter), a fed worm settles, and a worm that fails to eat starves to death — its brain, body and mouth stop, and the corpse lies in the dish until the generation ends. Every death writes a post-mortem: when, where, how long since the last meal, and what the worm had learned. Eating is survival, enforced by the environment rather than the judge.

Learning within a lifetime

Two mechanisms let a single worm change during its own life, both modelled on measured C. elegans biology, and neither is inherited — a newborn starts from its genome, like real life.

  • Plasticity. Synapses whose two neurons fire together are briefly marked; when the worm finds food, the marked synapses strengthen (a dopamine-flavoured three-factor rule). The circuit that led to a meal becomes a habit; unused learning decays back toward the genome.
  • Boredom. Each nose neuron keeps a running average of what it has been smelling and responds only to the difference — habituation, the best-documented form of worm learning. A smell that just sits there fades to nothing in about ten seconds, so a worm parked on a stale patch loses interest and wanders toward fresher words. Eating snaps the nose awake again.

The knobs of both mechanisms — how fast to learn, how fast to forget, how hard hunger drives the senses, how quickly boredom sets in — are genes: evolution tunes not just what a lineage knows but how it learns.

Evolution, and the judge

A generation lasts one pass of the flask's text (~2 hours). At rollover, each worm's poem is scored by a language-model judge reading sampled 15-word windows for emotional impact and coherence — the rubric names the flask's own text, so a Beowulf worm is judged as a Beowulf poet, and 道德經 poems are judged in Classical Chinese. Fitness is quality per window, not volume: eating more buys nothing (hunger already compels eating); only writing better pays. The best genomes seed the next generation under a natural evolution strategy, and the cycle repeats, unwatched, indefinitely.

The connectome itself

The connectome graph is a faithful port of the GoPiGo / Busbice C. elegans network — the 302 neurons of the real animal, with their actual synaptic weights. Integrate-and-fire dynamics: each neuron accumulates incoming signal, fires when it crosses a threshold, sends its outgoing weights forward.

The motor neurons drive 34 muscles (17 dorsal, 17 ventral) which steer an IK chain head. The brain ticks at 2 Hz, the body at 60 Hz, both off a deterministic tick counter so the trajectory is exactly reproducible given (weights, seed).

Determinism, persistence, save points

Each worm has:

  • A unique random seed that initializes its connectome activity.
  • Its own copy of the connectome weights file (frozen in this version; the data layout already supports per-worm mutation in future versions).
  • A poem file (poem.txt) that appends every eaten word as it eats it.

Given the same seed + weights, two runs produce identical eaten-word sequences. The sim has no wallclock dependencies internally; it advances by integer body-tick counts. A determinism test in the repo enforces this.

Why parallel worms?

Within a flask, ten worms produce ten divergent readings of the same text — same connectome graph, same words drifting past, different genomes. Across flasks, the same machinery reads four different books; the Tao Te Ching even runs twice, once in Legge's English and once in the original characters — a controlled experiment in whether evolution writes different poets in different languages.

Hosting

The server is a single uvicorn + FastAPI process running forty worms on one shared loop at 60 Hz. Each WebSocket subscriber gets one of three feeds: a 10 Hz overview stream (for the gallery grid), a 60 Hz focus stream (only when you've zoomed into a worm), and an event stream that fires once per eaten word (for the poems page). A given worm's full state is only serialized when at least one viewer has clicked into it.

The public domain (wordswordsworms.org) is served through a Cloudflare Tunnel. No incoming firewall ports, no public IP, free TLS. Both uvicorn and cloudflared run as systemd services and survive reboots.

What this is not

  • Not a research tool. It's an art piece that happens to use real neuroscience as its substrate.
  • Not proven learning. The machinery for learning now exists at both timescales — within a life and across generations — but whether any lineage has demonstrably learned to write better is the open question the project exists to ask. Several promising curves in its history turned out to be measurement artefacts; we say so when it happens.
  • Not a chatbot, an LLM, or a generative model in the usual sense. The "writing" is the worm's wandering trajectory through a field of Shakespeare, intersected with whatever its brain happens to do.
